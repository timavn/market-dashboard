# Balance review and account questions

Read `get_balance_context` for current account facts, `get_portfolio_context` for holdings/exposure or the review's independent holdings cross-check, and `get_process_context` for a process-limit assessment. A Balance Review does not need unrelated market quotes or research and can be assessed on weekends and holidays.

Keep account equity, cash and settlement, collateral requirements, capacity, exposure, and interest evidence distinct. Keep separately verified account fields when marks or another provider fail. Historical accounting rows remain dated evidence; they cannot replace a failed current read.

## Choose the requested output

- **Additional issues below an existing Balance Review:** Follow the existing narrative request: only `## Balance issues` with normally one to three brief bullets, at most 150 words. Each issue names a concrete evidenced discrepancy, risk, or failure and its practical consequence. Do not repeat issues or numbers already shown above unless fresh evidence changes them. If checks are complete with nothing additional, use the specified no-additional-issues sentence. Never claim an all-clear on an incomplete check.
- **A normal current balance question:** Answer first, then a small `Metric | Value | Account implication` table only when it helps. Include only the metrics the question needs; put the evidenced observation time next to the value. Explain borrowing or capacity without treating buying power as a proposed budget.
- **A comparison between dates or snapshots:** Read [account-comparisons.md](references/account-comparisons.md) and use its dated five-column table. A retry refreshes the applicable current sources and retains the same requested comparison.

Negative accrued interest or a short position alone does not prove excessive costs or risk. Evidence needs comparable verified costs/rates, explicit limits, or a documented breach. Margin collateral is not a loan; NLV change alone is not investment P&L. Keep unsupported analytical fields as `—` and avoid generic advice.
